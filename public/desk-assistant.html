How it works · privacy, background behavior & limitations

Eye closure: MediaPipe tracks facial landmarks locally. We calculate EAR as (two vertical eye distances) / (two × horizontal distance) using pixel coordinates, averaged across both eyes. A timestamp-based timer triggers after continuous low EAR. A small hysteresis band reduces jitter.

Background behavior: Monitoring uses a timer rather than requestAnimationFrame so it can attempt to continue in hidden tabs. Browsers still throttle timers, pause camera frames, freeze tabs, or suspend devices. Gaps longer than 1.5 seconds invalidate continuity, rather than treating missing time as closed eyes. A frozen page cannot sound a new alarm or update its own status. Picture-in-picture and screen wake lock are conveniences, not guarantees. For dependable always-on behavior, a native desktop companion is required.

Expression matrix: Happy = mouth smile; stressed = brow lowering and mouth press; surprised = jaw opening and eye widening; tired = low EAR; neutral = complement of strongest signal. Percentages are smoothed rule scores, not evidence of internal emotion. No actual concentration score is inferred.

Quality checks: Poor lighting, large head turn/roll, small faces, multiple faces, and stale video disable eye-closure timing. Glasses, occlusion, facial differences, and backlighting can still cause false positives or missed detections. Calibrate while seated; do not rely on this as a medical or safety device.

Privacy: No video recording, analytics, storage, or uploads. Initial model, JavaScript, and WASM downloads contact jsDelivr and Google Cloud Storage; those hosts see ordinary connection metadata but not camera frames. Everything else runs in memory. Export is explicit and contains settings, sampled EAR history, and events—not images. This file needs internet for its initial vision dependencies.

Setup: Serve this file over localhost or HTTPS. Grant camera permission. In an embedded preview, open the page directly if camera or picture-in-picture permission is blocked. Test sound at a comfortable volume. The demo simulates prolonged closure between seconds 12 and 16 of a 22-second cycle.
